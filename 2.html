Roadmap to Low-Level Computing

Step 1: Understanding Transistors and Basic Digital Logic

Goal: Grasp how transistors work and how they combine to form logic gates.

  1. Learn the Basics:
    • Study how a transistor works (MOSFETs and BJTs).
    • Understand how transistors are used to create logic gates (AND, OR, NOT).
    • Resources: Digital Design and Computer Architecture (Chapter 1), Falstad Circuit Simulator.
  2. Hands-On Practice: Build logic gates (AND, OR, NOT) on a breadboard using transistors.
Milestone: Successfully implement basic logic gates and test their functionality.

Step 2: Combinational and Sequential Logic

Goal: Understand how logic gates form complex circuits like adders and memory units.

  1. Combinational Logic:
    • Learn about multiplexers, decoders, and adders.
    • Example: Build a 4-bit adder using XOR and AND gates.
  2. Sequential Logic:
    • Study flip-flops (SR, JK, D, T) and how they store data.
    • Learn how counters and shift registers work.
  3. Simulate: Use Logisim or Digital to simulate and verify circuits.
Milestone: Design and simulate a 4-bit binary counter.

Step 3: Building a Simple CPU

Goal: Understand the architecture of a basic CPU.

  1. Learn CPU Basics:
    • Understand the CPU components: ALU, registers, control unit, and memory.
    • Learn how instructions are fetched, decoded, and executed.
  2. Hands-On: Take the Nand2Tetris course and design a CPU using HDL.
Milestone: Complete the CPU design and execute a simple program.
General Tips:
  • Take notes at every step to solidify your understanding.
  • Break tasks into smaller, manageable units.
  • Join communities like r/OSDev and engage in discussions.
